What PulseSoc is

Most platforms make you leave to buy something. You find a person whose taste you trust, or a video that shows you a thing working, and then the trail goes cold: a different app, a different search box, a product page with no idea how you got there.

PulseSoc is built the other way round. The feed, the reels, the live video, the conversations and the marketplace are surfaces of one network, so attention can travel in a straight line — from people, to content, to conversation, to discovery, to commerce — without starting over at each step.

It began as a crypto-intelligence and scam-awareness product, and that inheritance is still visible in a few places on purpose: the safety posture, the education habit, and a scam-report post type that turned out to make more sense as something you share than as a tool you open. What PulseSoc is now, though, is a social network with commerce built into it.

The social network

The foundation is people. You follow the accounts you want to hear from, and a profile is the page someone lands on when they are deciding whether to follow you — your posts, your reels and your links in one place.

Replies nest, so an exchange between two people stays readable to everyone who finds it later instead of flattening into one long list. Posts can be liked, reposted to your own followers, and saved for yourself. You choose who sees each post as you publish it — public, followers only, or just you — and changing your mind about one post leaves the others alone.

The feed is also not a single list. Following and Friends show you the accounts you picked and nothing else; the topic tabs beside them change what you are reading about rather than who you are reading. More about the feed.

Create and share

A post can be plain text, a photo, a video, a GIF, a poll other people vote in, or a scam report — six kinds, from the same button. Statuses are their own surface for the lighter thing you want to put up today, with music, reactions and replies.

Beyond that there is short vertical video, long video, and going live from your phone. You can shoot in the app or bring something you already made.

Reels, video and live

Reels are short vertical video with sound, in a full-screen feed — the place where someone who does not follow you yet is most likely to meet you. More about reels.

Live video is the camera and microphone on your phone, and a stream is not gone when you stop: it stays watchable afterwards, and you can hand part of a broadcast to someone else while it is running. People watch and comment in it as it happens. More about live video.

Conversations and communities

Discovery is only worth something if it can turn into a conversation. Direct messages are one-to-one or group, and carry photos, voice notes and files as well as text. You can call anyone you can message, and an incoming call rings like a phone call rather than arriving as a notification you find later. More about messages.

For more than a handful of people there are two different shapes: topic groups, which are written down and stay, and live audio rooms, which are not. More about groups and rooms.

Discovery

A following feed only ever shows you what you already chose. Search works across people, posts and products, the topic tabs surface what the network is reading, and reels are a discovery surface by design — which is how an account with no followers yet gets found at all.

The same machinery suggests products, and it is the hinge this whole page turns on: a recommendation can appear next to the content you were already looking at, because the catalogue and the feed are part of the same system. You can turn social product suggestions off in settings; the marketplace itself keeps working either way.

Social commerce

Ordinary online shopping starts when you already know what you want: you search, you land on a product, you check out. That works, and it is a narrow door — it can only ever sell you the thing you could already name.

On PulseSoc the path can start earlier. Content leads to discovery, discovery leads to a product, the product sits in the marketplace, and the purchase happens there. Nobody has to go looking for a category page. The point is not that commerce is nearby; it is that it is connected to the reason you were here.

The marketplace

The marketplace is where commerce actually happens — one catalogue, one product page per item, and the canonical record of what is for sale. Anything shoppable anywhere else on PulseSoc points back to a listing here, so a product has one price and one description rather than a different answer on every surface.

You can browse and search it, open a product, choose its options, save it, add it to a cart and check out. Browsing and the cart do not require an account, so a link someone sends you opens to the product rather than to a sign-up form. Open the marketplace.

Sellers list and publish products into that same catalogue and reach buyers through the network rather than through a separate storefront nobody visits. Cash and local pickup work on any listing, a card option appears once the seller has finished payment setup, and which methods a listing accepts is shown at checkout before you enter any details. More about the marketplace covers payment in detail.

Some of what you see in the feed is surfaced by PulseDrop, a clearly-labelled automated account that highlights marketplace products in PulseSoc content. It is a curator and nothing more: it does not hold stock, it is not the seller, and it is not who you buy from. Every product it points at is an ordinary listing by an ordinary seller.

Intelligence and safety

PulseSoc has an assistant and a briefings surface that summarise and explain things — market context, what changed, what a term means. They are a layer that supports the experience, not the product itself, and they are there to help you understand something rather than to tell you what to do with your money. Nothing here is financial advice.

On safety, the useful things are the ordinary ones: you can report content and accounts, block people, choose who sees each post, and control what your profile shows. Reports reach human moderation. Scam awareness is part of the product rather than a promise about it — the scam-report post type exists so that what one person was approached with becomes something others can recognise.

We will not tell you that software keeps you safe, or that we detect every scam. Neither is true, and a safety claim you cannot keep is worse than no claim at all.

What PulseSoc does not do

Stating these is cheaper for everyone than letting you discover them.

Messages are not end-to-end encrypted
They are encrypted in transit — the connection between your phone and our servers is protected the way a banking site's is. That is not the same as us being unable to read them. Messages are stored in a form we can access, which is what makes it possible to act on a report or restore an account. If you need end-to-end encryption, PulseSoc is not the right tool for that conversation.
Calls are between two people
Group calling is not something you can use today, and there is no screen sharing on a call or in a live stream.
There are no hashtags or @-mentions
Nothing parses either one, so a # or an @ in a post stays plain text. Search and the topic tabs are how things get found.
Profiles have no story highlights
There is no highlights row to pin to the top of a profile.

Our mission

To build a more connected kind of social commerce — where relationships, creativity, conversation, discovery and buying are part of one experience instead of five disconnected ones — while the people using it keep meaningful control over their privacy, their safety and what they see.

That second half is the constraint, not the footnote. A network that connects discovery to commerce is only worth using if the person at the centre of it is still the one deciding.
